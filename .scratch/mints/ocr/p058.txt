The calculus has eight logical rules, namely, two rules for each connective c: One
rule introduces it to the succedent, and it is called (→ c) or c-succedent; the
second rule introduces c in the antecedent, and it is called (c=→) or c-antecedent.
Contraction contr and weakening weak are structural rules.

and all premises.

Sequents written over the line in a rule are called premises; the sequent under
the line is the conclusion of the rule. A formulaexplicitly shown in the conclusion
of the rule and containing the connective introduced by the rule is called the
principal formula. Thus & is the principal formula for &-rules,  V  for
V-rules, and so on. The subformulas , bof the main formula explicitly shown
in the premises are side formulas. Formulas in the lists F, △ are parametric
formulas; the formula  in the cut rule is the cut formula.
