4.1.
BHK-Interpretation
Let us recall Brouwer's requirement of effective existence: To claim 3rα, we
we must point out which of the disjuncts is true, that is, select an i E {o, 1} and
justify ai.
We describe now a "programming language"' and a method of mechanically
extracting programs in this language from intuitionistic proofs. This language
is a version of simply typed lambda calculus [2]. It is based on a semantics
of intuitionistic logic in terms of constructions, presupposing that any formula
A encodes a problem, and corresponding construction c solves this problem or
realizes A. We give an informal explanation of semantics of logical connectives
that evolved from the work of Brouwer, Heyting, and Kolmogorov. It is called
BHK-interpretation. We write crα for “c realizes α"' or "'c is a construction for
α":

iff c is a function and for every d, if drα then c(d)rp,

The last clause is equivalent to:
cr-α iff -drα for every d.
We see that our language for such constructions should include pairing, pro-
jections (components of pairs), injection into a direct sum, forming functions,
and applying functions to arguments.
